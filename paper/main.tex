\begin{abstract}
Let $s(n)$ denote the least side length of a square that contains $n$ pairwise
interior-disjoint unit squares, with arbitrary orientations.  We prove by an
exact computer-assisted base certificate together with a finite
analytic transport argument that
\[
  \boxed{s(17)>
  \frac{46129999999859}{9997499999900}}
  =4.614153538416645696808\ldots.
\]
The base certificate places a nonnegative rational measure in a square of side
$L=4.613$ and excludes seventeen squares of side $A=3999/4000$.  It is
\emph{parent aware}: for each continuous parent-angle interval it selects a
closed core strictly inside the parent and checks that core only over the
complete center domain attainable by legal parents in that interval.  The
measure has 3,280 atoms in 417 $D_4$-orbits, total mass $16.999991644$, and
every selected core has mass at least $1.000000030$.  A recentering theorem
then reuses these already certified core-center domains for slightly smaller
parents and a slightly smaller container, yielding the displayed stronger
rational bound without a new translation sweep.  Finally, we generalize the
charging language from point measures and threshold atoms to
\emph{intersection-trigger} charges, which compile finite overlap
incompatibilities into budgeted spatial resources while retaining a
two-dimensional event representation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and current interval}
For $L>0$ write $\Q_L=[0,L]^2$.  Let $s(n)$ be the minimum $L$ for which $n$
pairwise interior-disjoint unit squares can be placed in $\Q_L$.  The minimum
is attained by compactness.  The best known $17$-square construction is due to
John Bidwell and has side
\[
  L_B=4.6755300936045509516\ldots
\]
according to the high-precision catalogue maintained by Ellsworth and the
historical survey literature\cite{Friedman2009,Ellsworth}.

Our main result is the following.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
No packing of seventeen unit squares exists in a square of side
\[
  S=\frac{46129999999859}{9997499999900}.
\]
Consequently
\[
  s(17)>
  \frac{46129999999859}{9997499999900}
  =4.614153538416645696808\ldots.
\]
\end{theorem}

Thus the currently certified interval retained by this repository is
\[
  4.614153538416645696808\ldots <s(17)
  \le 4.6755300936045509516\ldots.
\]
The lower-bound proof is independent of any assumption that a near-optimal
packing resembles Bidwell's construction.

The classical unavoidable-point method is the integral special case of the
weighted method used below.  If every unit square contains one of sixteen
points strictly in its interior, then seventeen disjoint interiors would need
seventeen distinct points.  The August 2026 certificates of Mira and Fort made
that argument completely finite by subdividing the three-dimensional pose
space and checking each leaf with exact integer arithmetic\cite{Fort,MiraRepo}.

Burns' proposal made the decisive relaxation: assign nonnegative weights to
many points, require every possible square to capture a prescribed amount of
mass, and keep the total mass below 17\cite{Burns}.  Massaccesi used linear
programming to improve the measure, and Levy turned the architecture into a
reusable exact event-cell framework, obtaining $4.59$\cite{Levy}.  The present
project's subsequent $4.607$ and $4.613$ certificates used the same fractional
principle with more refined support and angular handling.

Guzhou0806's R012 certificate identified a further overconstraint.  An
auxiliary core may fit close to the wall even though the larger parent square
from which it is supposed to arise cannot fit there.  R012 therefore checks a
core only over the complete center domain attainable by parents in the
corresponding angle interval and allows the core direction and size to vary
with that interval\cite{Guzhou}.  The theorem below formalizes that reduction
and is the proof interface used by our final certificate.

For source links, verification status, and a more detailed division between
direct dependencies and parallel historical work, see the repository file
\path{CONTRIBUTORS.md}.

\subsection{Stage I: unavoidable points and exact pose-space subdivision}

Green's classical argument places sixteen witnesses so that every admissible
unit square meets at least one of them.  The proof is then a pigeonhole
argument: seventeen disjoint interiors cannot be assigned seventeen distinct
witnesses from a set of size sixteen.  The weakness is geometric rather than
combinatorial---one must prove a universal statement over all translations and
orientations of one square.

The 2026 exact-point certificates, including Brandwijk's $4.45$, Mira's
$4.450837$ and later $4.468292$, and Fort's $4.456575$, attack precisely this
universal geometric step.  They partition the three-dimensional pose space
(center $x$, center $y$, orientation) into finitely many rational boxes and
certify each box either infeasible or pierced by a witness point.  Fort's work
is especially useful historically because it independently optimized the same
basic exact architecture.  These results differ numerically but share the same
integral counting invariant:
\[
  \text{total witness budget}=16<17.
\]

\subsection{Stage II: fractionalize the witness budget}

Burns' weighted formulation replaces the discrete set of sixteen unit witnesses
by a nonnegative atomic measure of total mass below $17$.  Every admissible
square must capture at least unit mass.  The pigeonhole principle is replaced
by additivity over disjoint interiors:
\[
  17
  \le \sum_{i=1}^{17}\mu(U_i)
  \le \mu(\Q_L)
  <17.
\]
This relaxation can place small amounts of mass at many locations and is
strictly more flexible than sixteen points of weight one.

Massaccesi showed that the support and weights could be chosen effectively by
linear programming, raising the numerical bound substantially.  The
anabologyco-maker certificate pushed the same weighted idea much farther with a
large exact orientation partition and an additional Lean-checked finite layer.
Levy then supplied a reusable generator and exact event-cell sweep, together
with an interval-based cross-check and later dilation machinery.  In our
subsequent continuation, finer angular control, mixed wall/interior support,
and dual-guided column generation raised the weighted method through
$4.607028598640\ldots$ and then $4.613028635886\ldots$.

Mathematically, all of these results retain the same global invariant:
\[
  \text{least charge per selected core}\times17
  >
  \text{total available budget}.
\]
The improvements come from making the finite coverage problem easier to
satisfy without increasing that budget.

\subsection{Stage III: make the core transport parent aware}

The remaining inefficiency is subtler.  Earlier weighted verifiers asked for
coverage of every auxiliary core position allowed by the core's own geometry.
But a core is not a physical packed square; it is only a device chosen inside a
larger parent.  Some core centers near the container wall cannot arise from any
legal parent at the corresponding orientation.

Guzhou0806's R012 exploits this distinction.  It partitions the parent-angle
continuum into intervals, chooses one safe core recipe per interval, and checks
coverage only over the union of centers attainable by the actual parents in
that interval.  This is a weakening of the verification obligation while
preserving the physical theorem.  The present work keeps that relaxation,
makes the interval/core selection adaptive, and then changes the underlying
measure once the frozen R012 measure reaches an exact obstruction.

This gives a precise sense in which the current proof \emph{subsumes} the
earlier methods: the final counting theorem still contains the classical
pigeonhole invariant, fractional weights contain the exact-point method as an
integral special case, and parent-aware transport removes a constraint that is
irrelevant to the physical packing problem.

\section{Parent-aware weighted certificates}

The counting argument has a slightly more general form that separates the
geometric question from the particular choice of atoms.

\begin{lemma}[Budgeted disjoint-core principle]\label{lem:budget}
Let $C(K)\ge0$ be a charge assigned to closed cores $K\subseteq\Q_L$.  Suppose
there is a global budget $\mathcal B$ such that every finite pairwise-disjoint
family of cores satisfies
\[
  \sum_i C(K_i)\le \mathcal B.
\]
If every legal parent square admits a closed core strictly inside it with
charge at least $\gamma$, and if
\[
  \mathcal B<n\gamma,
\]
then $n$ pairwise interior-disjoint parents cannot fit in the container.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose one certified core from each hypothetical parent.  Since every core is
a closed subset of its parent interior, the selected cores are pairwise
disjoint.  Their total charge is at least $n\gamma$, contradicting the budget.
\end{proof}

Ordinary point measures are the simplest realization: if
$\mu=\sum_jw_j\delta_{p_j}$, set $C(K)=\mu(K)$ and
$\mathcal B=\mu(\Q_L)$.  The same lemma also contains Levy's threshold atoms.
For a finite set $S$, integer $k\ge1$, and weight $w\ge0$,
a threshold atom $(S,k,w)$ contributes $w$ to $C(K)$ when
$|K\cap S|\ge k$.  For pairwise-disjoint cores, the traces $K_i\cap S$ are
disjoint, so at most $\lfloor |S|/k\rfloor$ cores can activate the atom.  Its
global budget is therefore
\[
  w\left\lfloor\frac{|S|}{k}\right\rfloor.
\]
Budgets add over independent point and threshold atoms.  The current
$4.614153\ldots$ certificate uses point atoms only; this more general statement
is included to make precise how the parent-aware transport composes with the
stronger charging rules already present in the literature.

We now specialize to a finite nonnegative measure on $\Q_L$.  Fix an outer
side $L$ and a parent side $A$, and let
\[
  \mu=\sum_j w_j\delta_{p_j}.
\]
We will associate to every legal parent square $P\subseteq\Q_L$ of side $A$
a closed core $K(P)$ satisfying
\[
  K(P)\subset\interior P.
\]
Different parent orientations may use different core sides and directions.

\begin{proposition}[Parent-aware counting principle]\label{prop:counting}
Suppose every legal side-$A$ parent square $P\subseteq\Q_L$ admits a selected
closed core $K(P)\subset\interior P$ with
\[
  \mu(K(P))\ge\gamma.
\]
If
\[
  17\gamma>\mu(\Q_L),
\]
then seventeen pairwise interior-disjoint side-$A$ squares do not fit in
$\Q_L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $P_1,\ldots,P_{17}$ had pairwise disjoint interiors, then their selected
closed cores would be pairwise disjoint sets.  Nonnegativity gives
\[
  17\gamma
  \le \sum_{i=1}^{17}\mu(K(P_i))
  =\mu\!\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{17}K(P_i)\right)
  \le\mu(\Q_L),
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

The usual weighted proof is the special case $A=1$, $\gamma=1$, with one
uniform family of cores.  Proposition~\ref{prop:counting} shows that neither
normalization is necessary: only the ratio between the least selected-core
mass and the total mass matters.

\subsection{A finite certificate theorem that subsumes the recent methods}

The computational proofs discussed in Section~\ref{sec:lineage} differ mostly
in how they establish the hypothesis of Proposition~\ref{prop:counting}.  It is
useful to isolate a single theorem that contains the current parent-aware
architecture and makes those differences explicit.

Let $G=D_4$ be the symmetry group of the outer square.  Fold the orientation of
an individual physical square into the interval $[0,\pi/4]$ using its own
quarter-turn symmetry and, when needed, a reflection of the container.  Let
$u=\tan(\phi/2)$ be the folded half-angle parameter.

\begin{theorem}[Finite parent-aware weighted certificate]\label{thm:finite}
Fix $n\ge2$, outer side $L$, parent side $A$, a finite nonnegative
$G$-invariant measure $\mu$ on $\Q_L$, and $\gamma>0$.  Suppose a finite
collection of closed half-angle intervals
\[
  I_j=[a_j,b_j]
\]
covers the full folded orientation range.  For each $I_j$ choose a rational
core half-angle $t_j$ and core side $B_j>0$.  Assume:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(C\arabic*),leftmargin=2.6em]
\item the intervals meet without gaps and cover every folded physical
orientation;
\item for every $u\in I_j$, the concentric side-$B_j$ square at direction
$t_j$ lies strictly inside the side-$A$ parent at direction $u$;
\item $D_j$ is a closed set containing every legal center of every side-$A$
parent whose folded half-angle lies in $I_j$;
\item every side-$B_j$ core at direction $t_j$ with center in $D_j$ has
$\mu$-mass at least $\gamma$;
\item $\mu(\Q_L)<n\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}

Then no packing of $n$ side-$A$ squares exists in $\Q_L$.  Consequently,
\[
  s(n)>\frac{L}{A}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take a hypothetical packing and fold each parent separately.  Condition (C1)
places its orientation in at least one $I_j$.  By (C2) choose the corresponding
closed core strictly inside that parent; by (C3) its center belongs to the
domain on which (C4) was verified.  $G$-invariance lets us unfold without
changing the core's charge.  The selected cores lie in pairwise disjoint parent
interiors, hence are disjoint sets.  Summing their charges gives
\[
  n\gamma\le \sum_i\mu(K_i)\le\mu(\Q_L),
\]
contradicting (C5).  Scaling a unit-square packing at side $L/A$ by $A$ would
produce the forbidden parent packing.  Finally, compactness of the feasible
packing space upgrades exclusion at the endpoint to the strict inequality.
\end{proof}

This theorem separates three logically different tasks: \emph{geometry of
transport} (C1--C3), \emph{finite coverage} (C4), and \emph{global counting}
(C5).  The sequence of recent improvements can be read as successively
weakening unnecessary requirements in those three tasks.

\paragraph{Sixteen unavoidable points.}
Take integer atom weights, total mass $n-1$, and prove directly that every
physical parent interior captures an atom.  This is the integral predecessor
of (C4)--(C5).  Mira's and Fort's exact pose-space trees differ from the present
proof mainly in how the universal coverage statement is finitized.

\paragraph{Uniform weighted cores.}
Burns, Massaccesi, Levy, and our earlier weighted certificates allow fractional
weights but use a common core rule over a finite orientation net.  The
strict-containment lemma transports each arbitrary parent to a nearby checked
core.  This is Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} with $D_j$ taken to be the full domain
where the auxiliary core itself fits.  That domain is safe but can be larger
than the set of centers realizable by physical parents.

\paragraph{Parent-aware cores.}
Guzhou's R012 observes that (C3) only needs a superset of \emph{legal parent
centers}, not every location where an auxiliary core happens to fit.  It also
lets $(t_j,B_j)$ vary from interval to interval.  The present theorem retains
that relaxation and adds adaptive interval refinement plus a new measure.

Thus the current proof does not merely place the earlier record values in a
timeline: it uses a theorem whose hypotheses expose exactly which constraints
each successive method removed.

\section{Continuous parent angles and exact center domains}
Represent a folded parent orientation by the half-angle parameter
\[
  u=\tan(\phi/2),\qquad 0\le u<1.
\]
Then
\[
  c(u)=\frac{1-u^2}{1+u^2},\qquad
  s(u)=\frac{2u}{1+u^2},
\]
and the axis-parallel width factor of a square at that orientation is
\[
  f(u)=c(u)+s(u)=\frac{1+2u-u^2}{1+u^2}.
\]
A side-$A$ parent with parameter $u$ can have its center precisely in
\[
  \left[\frac{Af(u)}2,L-\frac{Af(u)}2\right]^2.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Complete parent-center envelope]\label{lem:domain}
For a closed parent-angle interval $[a,b]\subset[0,1)$, the union of all legal
parent-center domains is the square
\[
  D_{a,b}=[r,L-r]^2,
  \qquad
  r=\frac A2\min\{f(a),f(b)\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A direct differentiation gives
\[
  f'(u)=\frac{2(1-2u-u^2)}{(1+u^2)^2}.
\]
Hence $f$ has one interior maximum and no interior minimum.  The legal center
squares are nested as their half-width changes, so their union is determined
by the minimum value of $f$, attained at an endpoint.
\end{proof}

For a candidate core with half-angle parameter $t$ and side $B$, put
$(c_t,s_t)=(c(t),s(t))$.  For a parent parameter $u$, its width across either
parent normal is bounded by
\[
  B\left(c_tc(u)+s_ts(u)
  +\abs{s_tc(u)-c_ts(u)}\right).
\]
On every retained interval the verifier checks that the relative angle stays
within $\pi/4$, so this factor is monotone with the absolute relative angle and
its maximum occurs at an interval endpoint.  The strict rational inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:contain}
  B\max_{u\in\{a,b\}}
  \left(c_tc(u)+s_ts(u)+\abs{s_tc(u)-c_ts(u)}\right)<A
\end{equation}
therefore proves that the concentric closed core lies strictly inside every
parent throughout the whole continuous interval.

The parent-center envelope matters quantitatively.  In the audited R012 data,
one fixed auxiliary core has minimum captured mass $0.985238$ if every center
where the core itself fits is demanded, but minimum $1.000288$ on the complete
legal-parent envelope.  The deficient all-core placement is unattainable by a
legal parent.  This exact ablation is retained with the current certificate.

\section{Exact translation sweep}
For one catalogue entry $(a,b,t,B)$, Lemma~\ref{lem:domain} supplies the exact
closed center square $D_{a,b}$.  Rotate center coordinates into the selected
core frame.  Each atom then contributes its weight exactly on a closed
axis-parallel square of side $B$ in center space.

The C++ verifier sweeps all vertical rectangle events and uses clamped
extrema of the rotated parent-center domain to determine each slab's reachable
vertical projection.  Within an open slab, the active rectangles
are fixed.  Horizontal events partition the reachable vertical projection into
open cells of constant charge.  Every positive-area reachable cell is checked,
and a rational point in every reported minimum cell is directly recounted.
All direction vectors are represented by rational half-angle parameters, and
all geometry is reduced to integer or rational comparisons.

Closed boundaries cannot conceal a lower charge.  Every boundary placement is
a limit of non-event interior placements.  Because only finitely many cells
exist, a subsequence approaches through one fixed cell; closed capture preserves
all atoms present in that cell at the limit, and nonnegative weights prevent a
decrease.  Thus the finite sweep proves the minimum over the entire closed
parent-center domain.

The certificate uses the eight symmetries of the outer square to store atoms in
$D_4$-orbits.  This constrains the measure only; a hypothetical packing is not
assumed symmetric.  Each parent is folded individually, assigned one catalogue
core, and then unfolded.


\section{Reusing certified coverage by recentering cores}\label{sec:recenter}

The parent-aware theorem above still chooses a core concentric with its parent.
That condition is convenient, but it is not part of the counting argument.
Only strict containment is required.  This observation lets us reuse an
already certified core library for nearby parent/container parameters without
assuming any continuity of the atomic measure.

Suppose a previously certified core shape $K_j$ has a certified center domain
$D_j$ and charge at least $\gamma$ for every translate $y+K_j$ with
$y\in D_j$.  For a centered parent shape $P$, define its
\emph{erosion domain}
\[
  E_j(P)=\{v:v+K_j\subset\interior P\}.
\]
A parent centered at $x$ can use this core entry exactly when there is
$y\in D_j$ with $y-x\in E_j(P)$, equivalently
\[
  x\in D_j-E_j(P).
\]
For centrally symmetric squares $E_j(P)=-E_j(P)$, so this is the Minkowski
sum $D_j+E_j(P)$.  Hence an entire legal parent-center set is covered whenever
it lies in the union of these translated erosion domains.

\begin{proposition}[Recentered-core transfer]\label{prop:recenter}
Assume a charge $C$ has budget $\mathcal B$ on pairwise-disjoint cores, and
that every translate $y+K_j$ with $y\in D_j$ has charge at least $\gamma$.
If, for every legal parent pose $P+x$, its center $x$ lies in
\[
  \bigcup_j(D_j-E_j(P)),
\]
then every parent can choose a strictly interior certified core.  If
$\mathcal B<n\gamma$, no packing of $n$ such parents exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose an index $j$ and $y\in D_j$ with $y-x\in E_j(P)$.  Then
$y+K_j\subset\interior(P+x)$ and has charge at least $\gamma$.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:budget} to the selected cores.
\end{proof}

Use one common container-centered coordinate frame for the old and new
domains; the new container lies inside the old one. The old measure and its
total budget remain available without moving any atoms.
For the square domains in the current certificate this condition becomes a
single exact scalar inequality.  Let the new parent have side $a$, angle
$\phi\in[0,\pi/4]$, and outer side $H$, and set
\[
  f(\phi)=\cos\phi+\sin\phi.
\]
Its legal centers form a square of half-side
\[
  q=\frac{H-af(\phi)}2.
\]
Suppose old core entry $j$ has side $B_j$, angle $\theta_j$, and certified
center domain $[-d_j,d_j]^2$.  Its width across either parent normal is
\[
  w_j(\phi)
  =
  B_j\bigl(
    |\cos(\theta_j-\phi)|+|\sin(\theta_j-\phi)|
  \bigr).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Exact square recentering criterion]\label{lem:square-recenter}
Assume $q\ge0$ and $d_j\ge0$. Every legal center of the new parent can use
entry $j$ if and only if
\[
  \boxed{
  w_j(\phi)
  +f(\phi)\,[H-af(\phi)-2d_j]_+
  <a.
  }
  \tag{R}
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  w_j(\phi)<a,\qquad
  H<
  af(\phi)+2d_j+\frac{a-w_j(\phi)}{f(\phi)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Clamp each coordinate of the parent center into $[-d_j,d_j]$.  Each coordinate
moves by at most $[q-d_j]_+$.  Projection of that displacement on either parent
normal is at most $f(\phi)[q-d_j]_+$.  Adding the core half-width gives
sufficiency of (R).

For necessity when $q>d_j$, put the parent center at $(q,q)$.  Every available
core center has both coordinates at most $d_j$, so its displacement projected
on the parent normal $(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$ is at least
$f(\phi)(q-d_j)$.  Thus no other recentering can improve the extreme corner.
When $q\le d_j$, only $w_j(\phi)<a$ is needed.
\end{proof}

This criterion is exact.  It reuses mass statements only at core centers that
were already certified; no continuity of $\mu$ under translation is assumed.

\subsection{A finite scalar sharpening of the base certificate}

For old catalogue row $j$ with parent interval $I_j$, define
\[
  m_j=\min_{u\in I_j}f(u),\qquad
  G_j=\max_{u\in I_j}g(t_j,u),\qquad
  \eta_j=A-B_jG_j.
\]
Its old certified center-domain half-side is
\[
  d_j=\frac{L-Am_j}{2}.
\]
Decrease the parent side and outer side to
\[
  a=A-\varepsilon,\qquad H=L-\delta.
\]
If $F_j$ is any upper bound for $f$ throughout $I_j$, then
Lemma~\ref{lem:square-recenter} is implied by the simpler rational condition
\[
  \boxed{
  \eta_j>
  \varepsilon
  +F_j[\varepsilon F_j-\delta]_+
  }
  \tag{S}
\]
for every row.  Indeed, the bracket bounds the extra center displacement
needed to clamp a new legal parent center back into the old certified domain,
while $\eta_j-\varepsilon$ is the remaining strict containment margin.

For the retained 4,391-row catalogue choose
\[
  \varepsilon=10^{-11},
  \qquad
  \delta=\frac{141}{10^{13}}.
\]
Using exact endpoint maxima for $F_j$ unless the interval crosses the maximum
of $f$, and the rational upper bound
\[
  \frac{1414213563}{10^9}>\sqrt2
\]
otherwise, all 4,391 inequalities (S) hold.  The minimum remaining scalar
slack is the positive rational
\[
\frac{
3156837929188515937426608335639966497291
}{

This decomposition is useful because the three gains come from different
sources.  The first removes a verifier overconstraint.  The second changes only
weights, showing that the parent-aware geometry exposed previously hidden
slack in the support.  The third changes the support itself, which becomes

With Guzhou's R012 measure held completely fixed, adaptive parent-aware core
selection was reported to raise the target to

  \frac{922600}{199979}=4.613484415863665\ldots.

An exact legal parent then shows why further angle refinement alone stops

\[
  \frac{461300}{99989}=4.613507485823440\ldots
\]
the entire parent captures only $0.998982$ mass, below the required common

parent itself.  This is a limitation of that fixed measure under the common
charge rule, not an upper bound on $s(17)$.

The final improvement therefore changes the measure.  Reweighting the inherited

the finite verifier, and improving the underlying measure.

The remaining gap to Bidwell is still substantial.  Other work in this
repository studies local optimality and compatibility-aware strengthenings, but
no global proof of Bidwell optimality is claimed here.


\section{Compatibility information as spatial charges}\label{sec:triggers}

The point-measure method prices each core through additive spatial mass.  That
relaxation can miss information about which individually admissible square
poses can coexist.  We now describe a budgeted charge that
converts finite overlap incompatibilities back into the same spatial
containment language used by the event sweep.

\subsection{Intersection-trigger atoms}

Let $T_1,\ldots,T_m$ be nonempty finite sets of spatial points satisfying
\[
  T_i\cap T_j\ne\varnothing
  \qquad(i\ne j).
\]
Define
\[
  C_T(K)
  =
  \mathbf 1\{\exists i:T_i\subseteq K\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Budget-one intersection trigger]\label{prop:trigger}
For every pairwise-disjoint family of closed cores $K_1,\ldots,K_r$,
\[
  \sum_{\ell=1}^r C_T(K_\ell)\le1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If two disjoint cores were charged, they would contain triggers $T_i$ and
$T_j$.  If $i=j$, both cores contain the same nonempty set.  If $i\ne j$,
both contain a point in $T_i\cap T_j$.  Either conclusion contradicts
disjointness.
\end{proof}

Levy's two-of-three threshold atom is a special case, obtained by taking the
three two-element subsets of a three-point set as triggers.  More generally,
if the triggers are not pairwise intersecting, their matching number---the
largest number of pairwise-disjoint triggers---is a valid global budget.  Thus
Lemma~\ref{lem:budget} simultaneously contains point measures, threshold atoms,
and these compatibility charges.

\subsection{Compiling an overlap clique into one spatial resource}

Suppose $m\ge2$ square poses $V_1,\ldots,V_m$ have pairwise-overlapping interiors.
Choose, for every pair, a common interior point
\[
  p_{ij}=p_{ji}\in\interior(V_i)\cap\interior(V_j),
\]
and define
\[
  T_i=\{p_{ij}:j\ne i\}.
\]
Then $V_i$ contains $T_i$, while
$p_{ij}\in T_i\cap T_j$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:trigger} therefore gives one
budget-one resource activating on the whole clique.  Strict overlap provides
open pose neighborhoods of activation, so this construction is not tied to
isolated exact poses.

The retained example in \path{certificates/intersection_trigger_29/}
contains 29 rational unit-square poses, 406 distinct pair witnesses, and 29
triggers of size 28. The minimum witness margin inside its required source
unit square is
\[
  \frac{314856211897415969021}{287142759881684900000000}
  >\frac1{1000}.
\]
Thus every trigger remains inside its corresponding concentric closed
side-$499/500$ core $K_i$, and $C_T(K_i)=1$ for all 29 cores.

The supplied nonnegative weights $\lambda_i$ satisfy
\[
  S:=\sum_i\lambda_i=\frac{81407286808}{79999999999},
  \qquad
  \max_x\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf1_{\interior V_i}(x)
  =m:=\frac{59431493389}{79999999999}.
\]
The checker verifies this maximum directly on the 29 poses. It enumerates all
vertex and edge-intersection abscissae, then all vertical interval cells in
each of the resulting 1,475 open slabs. The covering set is constant in each
cell. A point on an arrangement boundary cannot have a greater open-square
load: every open square containing that point also contains a neighbourhood
of it. A rational maximum witness is directly recounted.

Since $K_i\subset\interior V_i$, the same pointwise upper bound holds for
the closed cores, including their boundaries. Therefore any nonnegative
measure $\nu$ dominating the trigger charge on these cores satisfies
\[
  S\le\sum_i\lambda_i\nu(K_i)
  =\int\sum_i\lambda_i\mathbf1_{K_i}\,d\nu
  \le m\nu(\mathbb R^2).
\]
Its total mass is consequently at least
\[
  \nu(\mathbb R^2)\ge\frac{S}{m}
  =\frac{81407286808}{59431493389}
  =1.3697668048682659\ldots>1,
\]
whereas the trigger resource has budget one. This proves a strict separation
on the supplied finite family. It uses only these 29 poses, without assuming
a load theorem for a larger source family, and does not change the numerical
bound on $s(17)$.

The source expansion also verifies 71 recorded unit-square activations with
weight sum $99656162199/79999999999$, of which 68 survive shrinking to
side $499/500$, with weight sum $96836866527/79999999999$. An independent
8,405-slab arrangement check on those 71 poses gives maximum pointwise load
$73959042636/79999999999<1$. This verifies the earlier 68-core separation
of $1.2104608316026308\ldots$ without assuming the full source-family load
claim; the normalized 29-pose argument above gives the stronger separation.

\subsection{The continuous geometry remains two-dimensional}

At a fixed core direction, rotate every point $p\in T_i$ to coordinates
$(u_p,v_p)$.  A side-$B$ core centered at $(u,v)$ contains the entire trigger
$T_i$ exactly when its center lies in the rectangle
\[
R_i=
\left[
\max_{p\in T_i}u_p-\frac B2,\,
\min_{p\in T_i}u_p+\frac B2
\right]
\times
\left[
\max_{p\in T_i}v_p-\frac B2,\,
\min_{p\in T_i}v_p+\frac B2
\right],
\]
with a reversed interval interpreted as empty.  Hence the charge is the
indicator of
\[
  R_1\cup\cdots\cup R_m.
\]
For $m$ triggers this is a union of at most $m$ rectangles at each
direction.  Standard
vertical-slab decomposition gives polynomial-size exact event geometry.  The
pieces remain one resource with one budget; decomposing the union must not
assign independent budgets to the pieces.

For a general finite overlap graph $G$, choose one shared interior witness per
certified overlap edge and let each vertex trigger contain its incident edge
witnesses. Give each isolated vertex a private interior witness as well, so
every trigger is nonempty. Charged disjoint cores select distinct, nonadjacent
vertices of $G$, so any
proved bound $\alpha(G)\le b$ yields budget $b$.  Cliques give $b=1$, while
odd cycles and more complicated graphs can encode compatibility information
not expressible by clique cuts alone.

The current numerical lower bound does \emph{not} use intersection-trigger
atoms.  This section supplies a general mathematical framework for
future certificates: the parent-aware and recentering theorems apply unchanged
to a total charge
\[
  C(K)=\mu(K)+\sum_r w_r C_{T^{(r)}}(K)
\]
with nonnegative weights $w_r$ and budget
\[
  \mu(\Q_L)+\sum_r w_r b_r.
\]
A future improvement requires proving the corresponding universal core-charge
lower bound.
